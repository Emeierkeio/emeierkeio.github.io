A CERN for AI: what the manifesto asks, what the EU did

The “CERN for AI” is a request from scientists, and so far the European Union has answered with a virtual institute. A twelve-point manifesto, proposed in Bologna on 9 October 2025 by Giorgio Parisi and Pierluigi Contucci, asks the European institutions for a European Research Center for Artificial Intelligence, independent and with a physical hub. The Commission has started RAISE, which connects existing resources with €107 million from Horizon Europe.

What does the manifesto ask for?

The manifesto asks the European institutions to create an artificial intelligence research centre that is independent of companies and governments. It was proposed at the round table “Towards a European Research Center for Artificial Intelligence”, held in Bologna on 9 October 2025. The text is published as a petition, started on 3 October 2025 and addressed to the European Commission and the Council of the European Union. On 14 November 2026 the page shows 5,397 verified signatures.

The promoters are Pierluigi Contucci, Giorgio Parisi, Francesco Camilli, Federica Gerace and Gabriele Sicuro. Among the first signatories the page lists Yoshua Bengio, Michael Bronstein, Geoffrey Hinton, Yann LeCun, Luciano Maiani, Marc Mézard, Sara Solla and Cédric Villani.

“we ask the European institutions to create a new European Research Center for Artificial Intelligence, to be conceived not as an industrial infrastructure but as a scientific and cultural initiative grounded in long-term vision, theoretical depth, and independence from corporate or political agendas.”

The text has twelve principles. These are the most concrete:

  • Basic research. Understand how AI works, its risks and how to reduce them.
  • People. A permanent core of researchers, with fellowships and visiting scientists.
  • Independence. A non-profit legal form, an independent scientific advisory board, freedom to publish and a diversified funding base.
  • Open models. Prioritise open-source development “when it does not endanger the public”.
  • A place. A lean staff, large computing resources and a physical hub where researchers meet.

The manifesto names no budget and no city. The word “CERN” does not appear in the text: the comparison with the physics laboratory in Geneva comes from the public debate.

What has the European Union done so far?

The European Union has started a virtual institute, RAISE, and has not decided to build a centre. There are four official steps.

  1. 18 July 2024. In her Political Guidelines 2024-2029, on page 10, Ursula von der Leyen writes: “I will propose to set up a European AI Research Council where we can pool all of our resources, similar to the approach taken with CERN.”
  2. 8 October 2025. The Commission publishes the European Strategy for AI in Science, which introduces RAISE (Resource for AI Science in Europe) and defines it as a virtual institute pooling talent and resources.
  3. 3 November 2025. In Copenhagen, Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen and Commissioner Ekaterina Zaharieva launch the RAISE pilot, funded with €107 million from Horizon Europe. The RAISE page lists €33 million in the 2025 work programme and €107 million in 2026-2027. The Strategy also sets aside €600 million from Horizon Europe for researchers’ access to computing power.
  4. 15 October 2026. In Dublin the Commission opens a pledging initiative for companies and foundations that support RAISE: money, computing, models, data, fellowships. The first pledges come from the Novo Nordisk Foundation, Nvidia, Elsevier, Sanofi, Frontiers, Mistral and Google DeepMind. The press release gives no amounts.

How do the two proposals differ?

The manifesto asks for a new institution, while RAISE connects resources that already exist. The manifesto describes a non-profit body with its own researchers, a physical hub and its own scientific governance. RAISE is funded through Horizon Europe calls and, since 2026, through voluntary contributions from private organisations. The Commission pages I read do not mention the manifesto, and the October 2026 press release says nothing about turning RAISE into a centre.

The distinction also helps when reading headlines. An Italian article from October 2025 presented the manifesto as the EU’s “plan”. The document is a petition by scientists addressed to the European institutions, which have not adopted it so far.

What has been said in the Italian Parliament?

The idea reached the floor before the manifesto. A search with ParliamentRAG in the floor records of the Camera dei deputati (Chamber of Deputies) and the Senato (Senate), from June 2024 to today, finds the expression in six speeches, five of them earlier than 9 October 2025. The quotes are in the original Italian:

  • 19 February 2025, Senate: the Minister for Universities and Research, Anna Maria Bernini, speaks of «una sorta di CERN dell’intelligenza artificiale» (a sort of CERN for artificial intelligence);
  • 19 March 2025, Chamber: for Nicola Fratoianni (Alleanza Verdi e Sinistra) what is needed is «un grande CERN europeo dell’intelligenza artificiale» (a great European CERN for artificial intelligence);
  • 20 March 2025, Senate: Lorenzo Basso (Partito Democratico) recalls Parisi’s idea of «un CERN per l’intelligenza artificiale» (a CERN for artificial intelligence);
  • 23 June 2025, Chamber, in the debate on the AI law: Alberto Pandolfo (Partito Democratico) proposes «un CERN italiano dell’intelligenza artificiale» (an Italian CERN for artificial intelligence), and Elisabetta Piccolotti (Alleanza Verdi e Sinistra) recalls that «la proposta era nel programma delle elezioni europee di Alleanza Verdi e Sinistra» (the proposal was in the party’s programme for the European elections);
  • 14 October 2026, Chamber: for Elly Schlein (Partito Democratico) investment is needed also for «un CERN europeo sull’intelligenza artificiale» (a European CERN on artificial intelligence).

The search does not find this expression in the speeches of the other groups. This is a fact about the words used on the floor and says nothing about what those groups think of the proposal.

What I think

For me the deciding point is the ninth principle, the one on open models. In the note on Parisi and a single way of thinking I wrote that a model speaks with one voice and that, if it is closed, nobody outside can check the choices of those who built it. A public centre helps plurality only if its models, data and evaluations can be inspected.

The manifesto leaves one exception open: open source “when it does not endanger the public”. My reading is that the value of the centre will depend on who decides that exception and under which rules, and that these rules should be written in its statute. I ask the same question about RAISE: will the models and data that companies make available be open to checks by the people who use them?

It is the same requirement I apply in my own work, on a much smaller scale: an answer about public life must be checkable against its source.
