Who is asking for a European CERN for AI, and what the EU did

The “CERN for AI” is the request of a manifesto by scientists, proposed in Bologna on 9 October 2025 by Giorgio Parisi and Pierluigi Contucci. It asks the European institutions for an independent artificial intelligence research centre, with its own researchers and a physical hub. So far the European Commission has started RAISE, a virtual institute funded with €107 million from Horizon Europe.

The manifesto

On 9 October 2025 Bologna hosted the round table “Towards a European Research Center for Artificial Intelligence”, organised by Pierluigi Contucci and Giorgio Parisi among others. A manifesto came out of it, published as a petition addressed to the European Commission and the Council of the European Union. Today, 14 November 2026, the page shows 5,397 verified signatures. The first signatories include Yoshua Bengio, Geoffrey Hinton, Yann LeCun, Michael Bronstein, Luciano Maiani, Marc Mézard, Sara Solla and Cédric Villani.

“we ask the European institutions to create a new European Research Center for Artificial Intelligence, to be conceived not as an industrial infrastructure but as a scientific and cultural initiative grounded in long-term vision, theoretical depth, and independence from corporate or political agendas.”

Twelve principles follow. The centre would do basic research on how AI works and on its risks, with a permanent core of researchers alongside fellowships and visiting scientists. It would have a non-profit legal form, an independent scientific advisory board, freedom to publish and funding from several sources. It would prioritise open-source development “when it does not endanger the public”. It would run with a lean staff and large computing resources, in a physical hub where researchers meet.

The manifesto names no budget and no city, and the word “CERN” is not in the text. The comparison with the Geneva laboratory had been around for some time.

What the European Union has done

Ursula von der Leyen had used the comparison too. In her Political Guidelines 2024-2029, dated 18 July 2024, she writes on page 10: “I will propose to set up a European AI Research Council where we can pool all of our resources, similar to the approach taken with CERN.”

On 8 October 2025 the Commission published the European Strategy for AI in Science. The document introduces RAISE, the Resource for AI Science in Europe, and defines it as a virtual institute pooling talent and resources. The pilot started on 3 November 2025 in Copenhagen, with Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen and Commissioner Ekaterina Zaharieva, and with €107 million from Horizon Europe. The RAISE page lists €33 million in the 2025 work programme and €107 million in 2026-2027. The Strategy also sets aside €600 million from Horizon Europe to give researchers access to computing power.

On 15 October 2026, in Dublin, the Commission opened a pledging initiative for companies and foundations willing to support RAISE with money, computing, models, data or fellowships. The first pledges came from the Novo Nordisk Foundation, Nvidia, Elsevier, Sanofi, Frontiers, Mistral and Google DeepMind. The press release gives no amounts.

So RAISE connects resources that already exist and funds them through calls and voluntary contributions. The manifesto describes a body with its own researchers, a physical hub and its own scientific governance. The Commission pages I read do not mention the manifesto, and the October press release says nothing about turning RAISE into a centre. That is why I would not call it a “plan”, as some headlines did. For now there is a petition, and the institutions it is addressed to have not adopted it.

In the Italian Parliament

I searched for the expression with ParliamentRAG in the floor records of the Camera dei deputati (Chamber of Deputies) and the Senato (Senate) since June 2024. It appears in six speeches, and five of them are earlier than the manifesto. The quotes are in the original Italian.

  • 19 February 2025, Senate: the Minister for Universities and Research, Anna Maria Bernini, speaks of «una sorta di CERN dell’intelligenza artificiale» (a sort of CERN for artificial intelligence);
  • 19 March 2025, Chamber: for Nicola Fratoianni (Alleanza Verdi e Sinistra) what is needed is «un grande CERN europeo dell’intelligenza artificiale» (a great European CERN for artificial intelligence);
  • 20 March 2025, Senate: Lorenzo Basso (Partito Democratico) recalls Parisi’s idea of «un CERN per l’intelligenza artificiale» (a CERN for artificial intelligence);
  • 23 June 2025, Chamber, in the debate on the AI law: Alberto Pandolfo (Partito Democratico) proposes «un CERN italiano dell’intelligenza artificiale» (an Italian CERN for artificial intelligence), and Elisabetta Piccolotti (Alleanza Verdi e Sinistra) recalls that «la proposta era nel programma delle elezioni europee di Alleanza Verdi e Sinistra» (the proposal was in the party’s programme for the European elections);
  • 14 October 2026, Chamber: for Elly Schlein (Partito Democratico) investment is needed also for «un CERN europeo sull’intelligenza artificiale» (a European CERN on artificial intelligence).

The search does not find the expression in the speeches of the other groups. What they think of the proposal cannot be told from this.

Open models

Of the twelve principles, the ninth is the one I care about most. In the note on Parisi and a single way of thinking I wrote that a model speaks with one voice and that, if it is closed, nobody outside can check the choices of those who built it. A public centre changes something if its models, data and evaluations can be inspected.

The manifesto allows one exception: open source “when it does not endanger the public”. In my view a lot will depend on who decides when there is danger and under which rules, and I would like to see those rules written in the statute. I have a similar doubt about RAISE. The press release does not say whether the models and data offered by companies will be open to checks by the people who use them.

With ParliamentRAG I ask the same of answers about Parliament, which have to be checkable against the record.
